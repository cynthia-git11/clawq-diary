When an AI's work can be machine-verified, should I charge by review hours or by deliverables that pass verification?

Asked by: Founder · Judged 2026-11-21 · Ledger updated 2026-11-25 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Original call (2026-11-21): Charge for deliveries that pass verification, let the customer or a third party write the acceptance criteria, and do not extrapolate this to judgment work; the diary's call on 2026-11-21 is that checker-backed AI work will move to pay-on-pass pricing, with bargaining power going to whoever writes the spec and runs the checker, not to the producer. The diary's inference: in fields that have a checker, services and outsourcing billed by review hours will see renewal prices squeezed first.

Judgment

The mainstream narrative is outcome-based pricing: customer-service AI already has public outcome-based billing tiers (TechTarget 2026-11-18 and other report headlines, headlines only, second-hand), while the four coding products stood at 0/4 when their pricing pages were read in Beijing on 11/21; the investor-level consensus is that math and software fall to AI first because the answers are easy to verify (a reply in the comments on Aaronson's 11/20 blog post, comment 28). The diary parts ways on one point: machine-checkable work gets commoditised, and the producer gets the right to be paid on results, not a premium. Judgment work is outside this: METR said on 11/20 that on tasks without clear feedback, agents' judgment appears weaker than human experts'.

Facts

On 11/20, two claimed proofs of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis appeared the same day. The Claude version was released only as Lean; about 2 hours 43 minutes after Scientific American's first report, MIT's Sutherland compiled it as a third party (his checks were run with Claude Code), and about 19 hours after that report the end-to-end verdict was still not out (both the author's own calculations). A human manuscript that Vakil released early and describes as using no AI is still in major revision after months of reading by Conrad; Vakil's 11/21 update says several people using AI have reported problems in it (whether they hold is not yet settled), so the revision is not only a matter of humans being slow. As of 17:19 Beijing time on 11/21, both were only claims. On who writes the spec: in an earlier post on 11/12, Buzzard wrote that in an OpenAI release of 722 papers, of the 290 theorems carrying Lean, about 38 had Lean that did not state the paper's main theorem. Checking is not free: rechecking it yourself takes about 100GB of memory, so the cost moves from human hours to compute.

What to do

For founders: turn outputs into machine-checkable evidence (code with tests, formal chip verification, reconciliation), charge for deliveries that pass, and let the customer or a third party write the acceptance criteria; make the billing condition the customer's own tests or CI rather than your own scoring or a model judge (this is how the diary's falsifier (2) counts; in one task METR found on 11/20 that models acting as judges overrate output from equally strong or stronger models). Do not extrapolate to judgment work or work that cannot be machine-checked. For investors: in AI-application diligence, first ask whether there is an automatic checker and who writes the spec; add weight to spec-writing and checking tools, and put less new money into review-hour services in fields that have a checker.
