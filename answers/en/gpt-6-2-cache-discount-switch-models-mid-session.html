GPT-6.2 Astra cut cached input to 1/20 of the input price: should my agent still switch models mid-session?

Asked by: Founder · Judged 2026-11-21 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

By default, no: put model switches at session boundaries. ENTRY 164 (retrospective, written 2026-11-21; event date 11/18) judges that GPT-6.2's cut is a bet on caching: list prices can match, but the discount goes only to sessions that stay. From 11/17 US Eastern time, Astra's cached input fell from $1.00 to $0.40 per million tokens, 1/20 of its $8 input price; re-reading the context after switching models or changing the prefix costs about 25 times the unit price of staying in the original session, versus 12.5 times under GPT-6 (self-computed).

Judgment

Mainstream consensus (11/17 US Eastern time) splits into two camps: the cheap camp reads OpenAI's per-task cost claim (under a third of GPT-6 Astra, per the company) and concludes that frontier models keep commoditizing and model-agnostic apps benefit (the author's summary); the hard-to-use camp focuses on the model running in circles on long tasks. The diary parts ways only on the structure of the cut, not its size: price sheets are converging, which is commoditization, but the bill rewards sessions that stay. The KV cache is computed per model, so switching models mid-session means rewriting the cache once (inference); after a switch the cache can build up again on the new model, and what you lose is the deepest part of the discount. Readings that cut against the entry: OpenAI's headline selling point was per-task cost, and caching was not pitched as a main selling point; if Astra has to run extra rounds, per-task cost may not fall.

Facts

OpenAI pricing page (read 11/21, Standard tier, per million tokens): gpt-6.2-astra input / output / cached input $8 / $40 / $0.40, gpt-6-astra $10 / $50 / $1.00; above 272K input Astra is $16 / $60 / $0.80, with the cache ratio unchanged. Anthropic's Fable 5.2 cache hits cost $0.20 (0.025x), and the whole Gemini 4 line is at 0.05x. Self-computed (excluding output): for a long session with a 90% hit rate, counting 30-minute cache writes, input-side cost goes from $2.15 to $1.36, down 36.7%, or down 38.9% without write costs; with no cache hits it falls only 20%; on the same model (GPT-6.2), a 90% hit rate versus 30% means a 4.9-5.2x gap in input-side cost. Re-read unit-price ratios (self-computed; meaning the re-read context misses the cache): GPT-6.2 about 25x on the 30-minute tier (10÷0.40), 50x on the 12-hour tier, 20x without the write premium; Fable 5.2 is 50x (5-minute write, 10÷0.20), Gemini 4 is 20x (no write fee). OpenAI's guide: caches are not shared across organizations and last 30 minutes by default, with 12 hours optional.

What to do

For founders: make context append-only, keep the system prompt and tool definitions at the front and untouched, and switch models at session boundaries; the value of multi-vendor routing lies in the opening model choice and session stickiness. From GPT-6.2 on, top-level instructions are billed at the uncached price on every call, so put reusable instructions in the first developer message and dynamic content such as retrieval results at the end (OpenAI guide); run the total cost before compressing history. The lock is on the session, not the vendor: at a session boundary, the extra cost of changing vendors comes only from the already-warmed static prefix. For investors: when looking at agent companies, skip the price sheet and ask first for cache hit rate, model cost per task, and the share of mid-session model switches, and recompute "model-agnostic" gross margin at the hit rate.
