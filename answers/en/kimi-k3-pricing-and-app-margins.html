Kimi K3 lists at $3/$15 per million tokens: can switching to it rescue an AI app's gross margin, and what are the catches?

Asked by：创始人·AI 应用 CEO / CTO · Judged 2026-09-29 · Ledger updated 2026-10-02 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Yes, but the diary's sample, Harvey, got there by post-training on a Kimi K3 base (the company's own statement), not by only repointing the API. Its gross margin went from about 50% early in the year to -50% in June and turned positive after it released its own model in August, per Bloomberg on 9/21 citing people familiar; Harvey declined to comment.

Judgment

The mainstream view is that if money moves, it runs to the cheapest model. The diary's reading is that buyers on the Vercel gateway stepped down only one price tier, and Kimi K3 at $3/$15 is a mid-tier price. The weights are open, but pricing power stays in the license and the official API, so the savings come with conditions.

Facts

Vercel model page, 9/29: Kimi K3 lowest price $1.325/$10.50, while most providers still charge $3/$15. Vercel export (a list-price-weighted estimate, not gateway billing): its spend share averaged 9.3% over the 30 days from 8/30 to 9/28 and peaked at 18.5% on 9/22, a pulsed pattern. Moonshot AI raised its official API output price from ¥27 to ¥100 per million tokens 11 days before the weights went live, about 3.7x (ENTRY 114 listed no source line; not independently recovered).

What to do

For founders: move agent workloads first, compute margin twice (at the gateway's lowest price and at the raised official price), open talks on the license revenue gate a year ahead, and state in customer contracts who generates the reply and where data goes. For investors: in diligence ask how much of the company's value disappears if the base model is swapped, and among Chinese models back the ones that can collect money above $10/M output.
