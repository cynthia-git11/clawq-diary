Investors now ask about training-data provenance: what should I prepare, and can a copyright suit really hurt a round?

Asked by：创始人·自研模型 / 生成式内容创业者 · Judged 2026-08-31 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Prepare corpus purchase records first; they are now part of the financing file. A copyright suit hurts a round through timing, destruction orders and founders' personal liability, while damages can be computed: the book case settled for $1.5B, 3109 dollars per work, only 2.07% of the statutory cap. This call is still in progress.

Judgment

The mainstream view watches the damages figure. The diary starts from the offering calendar: applying the same ratio to the music side gives roughly two ten-thousandths of Anthropic's $965B post-money valuation, which barely moves valuation. What moves the window and the price range is a contingent liability that cannot be quantified.

Facts

On 8/28, 35 publishing entities of Sony and Warner sued Anthropic and named two founders as individual defendants (not the first such case). The book case settled for $1.5B over 482460 works; 3109 dollars per work is a gross figure and a price negotiated after liability was found. Scope note: the two cases are not comparable, and liability in this case is not yet established. Reconciliation on 9/25: no filing by the company itself on EDGAR; WSJ on 9/18 (as relayed by Investing.com) reportedly put the listing target at November, with people familiar saying it is unrelated to the litigation.

What to do

For founders: put corpus purchase records into the financing file rather than leaving them with legal. For investors: watch the order of the public S-1 filing date and the settlement date, and ask first about destruction orders and founders' personal liability, neither of which is priced per work.

Falsifier (verbatim from the diary footnote)

Within 12 months from 2026-08-31, if a second court-approved public settlement appears with a per-work price within one order of magnitude of 3109 dollars, then "damages do not move valuation" holds and is upgraded to verified; if Anthropic delays or withdraws its offering because of this group of lawsuits, the main call is marked ✓; if the public S-1 is filed on schedule and the offering is unaffected, this call is downgraded. Adjudication sources are the court's final approval documents and SEC EDGAR.
