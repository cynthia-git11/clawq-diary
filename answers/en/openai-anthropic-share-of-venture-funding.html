How much of global VC went to OpenAI and Anthropic, and should LPs keep funding AI megarounds?

Asked by：投资人·LP · Judged 2026-08-08 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Do not buy beta at the consensus price while still paying VC fees; megarounds need their own fee schedule. Global venture funding in the first half was $510 billion; OpenAI plus Anthropic took $217 billion, 43% of it (committed basis, single source), and with neither listed so far their DPI contribution is zero.

Judgment

The mainstream answer is 'don't invest in consensus'. The diary calls that line too cheap; the hard part is not buying beta at the consensus price while still paying VC fees. Wellington says mega AI exposure may deliver only beta-like returns: you pay active-management fees and buy an index.

Facts

Crunchbase (7/2): $510 billion of global venture funding in the first half; OpenAI plus Anthropic $217 billion, 43%, on a committed basis with $35 billion of it contingent, single source. PitchBook-NVCA: 2021-vintage US VC funds average 0.05x five-year DPI while one-year IRR is 17.1%. The two companies filed confidential S-1s a week apart and neither has listed; OpenAI's compute commitments through 2030 are about $600 billion.

What to do

For founders: 43% is two companies' fundraising, so do not read it as your market's temperature; design an employee liquidity plan that does not depend on an IPO now. For investors: give megarounds their own fee schedule; model exits on private holdings as 'in the queue', and watch the 90 days after the first listing.

If within two years the 2021 vintage's seven-year DPI recovers to 0.3x or above, this entry is downgraded.
