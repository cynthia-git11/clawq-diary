After Jensen Huang's resignation, will Nvidia keep guaranteeing and investing in AI clouds, and will Nvidia-backed financing get more expensive?

Asked by: Investor · Judged 2026-11-21 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

The diary's judgment (retrospective, written 2026-11-13): Nvidia's signed guarantees and equity stakes run as agreed, new ones tighten first, and AI clouds that borrow against new Nvidia backstops will pay more for their next debt (an inference). As of Nov 21 there was still no permanent CEO and Kress remained interim CEO (ENTRY 163, retrospective, written 2026-11-21).

Judgment

The consensus applies a key-person discount to Nvidia: CNBC on Nov 12 tallied 6 rating downgrades, the media read Nvidia as "the AI central bank", and Fortune on Nov 12 quoted Joe Kaiser saying Nvidia's guarantees are no longer worth what they were a week earlier (secondhand). The diary prices the existing book and new deals separately: the guarantees are contractual obligations of the company itself, with total assets of $320.27 billion, and do not leave with the CEO; what shrinks is new business, so the discount falls on the next financing that needs fresh Nvidia backing. Second order (6–18 months): AI clouds and compute lessors that got cheap debt on new Nvidia backstops will see their next debt priced as unsecured, and financing costs rise (an inference; the basis is Fortune on Nov 12 quoting Kaiser that an Nvidia backstop lowers neoclouds' borrowing rates, secondhand). The reasons for "new ones tighten first" (an inference): the interim CEO is also the CFO, with a first-ever chair, Jones, above her, and the independent investigation the board opened on Nov 8 had announced no findings as of Nov 12. ENTRY 163 adds the succession layer: the successor has to clear licensing and investigation risk first (an inference); on Nov 21 the interim team was still in place, and Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate Huang (as reported). The diary also concedes that "Kress will tighten new deals" is close to consensus, and that the tightening began before Huang left: the Ohio guarantee was reported at up to $250 billion during negotiations (CNBC, WSJ, Jul 26–27) and had been scaled back by Aug 14 (Reuters citing WSJ).

Facts

Nvidia press release and 8-K (Nov 12): Huang resigned as president, CEO and director, CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO, and the board said it expects customer commitments to continue without interruption. NVDA closed down 4.3% that day in New York, but it had already fallen 19.3% from the Oct 28 close to the Nov 11 close and was down as much as 13.1% at the Nov 12 open (all author's calculations), so a large part of the key-person discount was priced in before the announcement. 10-Q (Aug 26, company figures): maximum guarantee exposure $108.5 billion, including the OpenAI lease guarantee signed in August; commitments to buy back AI cloud capacity $36 billion; equity investment commitments $25 billion. Base rate: from Mar 2 to Nov 10 Nvidia signed at least 4 new deals of this kind of $1 billion or more, about 0.5 a month (author's calculation). Financing cost has readings only: CNBC said CoreWeave's CDS rose 94% from Oct 1 to Nov 5, before the event. On Nov 17 Nvidia set up a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee and stopped shipping licensed Hopper products to China (Q3: $2.4 billion, about 2.1% of revenue; company figure, share is author's calculation).

What to do

For investors: count signed Nvidia guarantees at contract value, turn "Nvidia will follow on and backstop again" into a contingency, and re-price a portfolio company's next financing as if there were no new guarantee; in diligence, separate supplier financing from customer revenue. For founders: if your compute provider is financed on new Nvidia guarantees, sign a second provider now.
