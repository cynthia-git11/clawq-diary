How should investors account for warrants compute vendors grant to AI labs, like Akamai's 5% to Anthropic?

Asked by：投资人·二级 · Judged 2026-09-25 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Treat the warrant as the vendor's customer-acquisition cost: deduct it from the contract value before applying a multiple. Akamai's 9/24 announcement: Anthropic commits to pay $11.6 billion over seven years for CPU compute, and Akamai issues warrants for up to about 5% of its shares (press-release basis) at a $111.33 strike.

Judgment

The consensus reads the $11.6 billion as revenue visibility for Akamai. The diary reads it as a vendor buying a customer with its own equity: strip the equity given away out of the contract value, and on Anthropic's side book the seven-year commitment as quasi-debt inside EV, a fixed seven-year outlay set against a Sol list price that reportedly halved in a single week.

Facts

Akamai 8-K (2026-09-24): Anthropic commits about $11.6 billion over seven years; on the press-release basis the warrants equal about 5% of shares outstanding at a $111.33 strike, about 2% vesting with existing commitments and about 1% per additional $3 billion of contract value. AMD 8-K (2025-10-06): warrants to OpenAI for up to 160 million shares at a $0.01 strike. As reported by VentureBeat (9/22), GPT-6 Sol's list price halved.

What to do

For investors: on any large compute deal, open the 8-K and look for warrants first; take the dilution out of the contract value before computing multiples, and book the model company's multi-year commitment as quasi-debt in EV. For founders: if you sell compute, count any equity the customer asks for as acquisition cost; if you buy compute, ask for warrants or a price reset before signing a multi-year commitment.
