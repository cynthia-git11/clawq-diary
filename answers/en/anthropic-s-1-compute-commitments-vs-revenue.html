How large are the compute commitments in Anthropic's S-1, and how do the next 12 months of compute bills compare with its revenue?

Asked by: Investor · Judged 2026-11-13 · Ledger updated 2026-11-25 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Ledger update 2026-11-25 (ENTRY 153): ⏳ Open; last reading 2026-11-25; reading note (Chinese only): 「截至北京 11/25，Anthropic 的 EDGAR 页面上 11/21 以来只有 4 份内部人 Form 4（11/23，Dario、Daniela Amodei 与董事 Liddell、Narasimhan 递交），公司没有递交定期报告，R 仍约 1.04（自算）」. Ledger update 2026-11-21 (ENTRY 153): ⏳ Open; last reading 2026-11-25; reading note (Chinese only): 「424B4 沿用约 9009 亿美元的承诺总额，12 个月内到期约 707 亿，R 仍约 1.04（自算）」.
Original call (2026-11-13): Anthropic's S-1 puts total commitments at $900.9 billion, of which about $70.7 billion falls due in the year from 9/30, roughly equal to annualized revenue: 1.04x Q3 GAAP revenue times four (about $67.8 billion) (retrospective, written 2026-11-13; S-1/A of 2026-11-10, author's calculation). The next year is covered: 9/30 cash plus short-term investments of $131.9 billion is 1.87x the 12-month commitments (author's calculation); the pressure is whether revenue growth outruns the $451.6 billion due by the end of 2030.

Judgment

The consensus formed on the event day: most of the loss is on paper and the commitments are huge (FT 10/26, 9fin 10/27), so the diary was not the first to look at the commitments. It parts ways on the measure: not the $900.9 billion total (about $449.3 billion falls in 2031 or later, author's calculation), nor media estimates of an annual bill or the company's Run-Rate, but the filing's 12-month amount divided by GAAP revenue times four (R), discounting ANT's revenue multiple by the direction of R. This also replaces the diary's 9/25 advice to fold "model companies' multi-year commitments into EV as quasi-debt": folding them in at full value double-counts compute costs already deducted in free cash flow. Of the $80.7 billion H1 net loss, $72.4 billion is fair-value changes on convertible notes and similar instruments: no cash out; the cost is dilution.

Facts

The original S-1 of 10/26 left the commitment figures blank; amendment No. 1 of 11/2 first filled them in ($895.4 billion, $69.8 billion); the entry's $70.7 billion, 1.04x and $131.9 billion all come from the 11/10 amendment, which the author did not know on 10/27. Of the $900.9 billion, $186.2 billion carries termination rights or conditions, and about $150–175 billion of arrangements without fixed amounts sit outside the total. Commitments to Google (at least $121.3 billion) and Amazon (at least $110 billion) must be made up if unused and are non-cancelable; both are also shareholders, and new IPO shareholders hold only the junior layer. Q3 compute and infrastructure costs rose back to 58% of revenue. Other measures: excluding the conditional part of about $4.2 billion R is about 0.98, and on the company's 10/31 Run-Rate of about $81 billion R is about 0.87 (all author's calculations); Q3 times four understates the exit revenue run-rate, so this choice pushes R up, in the entry's favour.

What to do

For investors: recompute ANT's "commitments due within a year ÷ GAAP quarterly revenue × 4" every quarter and compress the revenue multiple while it is above 1; in a model-layer company's new round, put the 12-month amount of non-cancelable compute commitments and GAAP revenue times four in one table, and where the ratio is above 1 negotiate termination and shortfall-payment clauses first (a diligence measure, not a valuation deduction). For founders: manage multi-model bargaining as gross margin; per The Information on 10/24 (single source, not confirmed by the company), Replit moved most of its AI spend from Anthropic to OpenAI.
