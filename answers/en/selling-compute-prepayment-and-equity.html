I sell compute: what's the minimum prepayment before I sign, and how do I account for a customer asking for warrants?

Asked by：创始人·新云 / 算力服务创始人 · Judged 2026-09-25 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Do not sign below one-fifth prepayment, and book the equity a customer asks for as customer-acquisition cost. Per Nscale's S-1, weighted-average customer prepayment is 23% and Anthropic's is zero; per Akamai's 9/24 press release, it issued warrants to Anthropic capped at about 5% of shares, strike $111.33.

Judgment

The mainstream reads neocloud risk as customer concentration. The diary reads it as how the contract allocates risk: the tenant prepays nothing, the supplier carries all the financing, and suppliers now issue equity to customers too. Contracted TCV is the tenant's option, and a large deal's contract value should be counted net of the equity given away.

Facts

Nscale S-1 (2026-09-18): weighted-average customer prepayment 23%; no fees on Anthropic's up to $44.6B tranches until acceptance. Akamai 9/24, press-release basis: Anthropic commits $11.6B over seven years; warrants for about 2% of shares vest with existing commitments, about 1% per additional $3B. AMD 8-K: on 2025-10-05 it issued OpenAI warrants for up to 160 million shares at a $0.01 strike.

What to do

For founders: do not sign below one-fifth prepayment; if a customer asks for warrants, count the dilution as customer-acquisition cost; lock the contract before buying cards, and do not pitch financing on GPU residual value. For investors: open the 8-K first on any large deal, deduct warrant dilution from contract value before computing multiples, and anchor valuation on activated plus financing-covered TCV.
