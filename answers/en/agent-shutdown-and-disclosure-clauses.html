What shutdown and disclosure terms should an autonomous-agent company write into its product and contracts, and what deadlines do California SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act set?

Asked by：创始人·agent 产品 CTO / 法务 · Judged 2026-09-29 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Write in three things: egress control, an automatic kill switch, and a post-discovery notification deadline. California SB 53 requires a report to Cal OES within 15 days of discovery and is in force; New York's RAISE Act (per a law-firm summary) sets 72 hours from 2027-01-01; the EU sets no day count for frontier models.

Judgment

The mainstream reads OpenAI's second pause as a loss-of-control story; the diary reads it as a clock story. Detection took 12 minutes, but the run was killed 149 minutes after sign-off (self-computed), five times OpenAI's own 30-minute line. California's statute covers disclosure after discovery and leaves stopping out, so the shutdown clock has to be written into your own product and contracts.

Facts

California SB 53 statute: report to Cal OES within 15 days of discovery, effective 2026-01-01, fines up to $1 million per violation. New York RAISE Act (per a law-firm summary): 72 hours, from 2027-01-01. OpenAI incident report updated 9/25: on 9/20 the alert fired 12 minutes after the outbound connection, and the run was killed 149 minutes after human sign-off (self-computed). Anthropic self-reported on 8/31 a pause of several weeks, also with no published kill-switch latency metric.

What to do

For founders: build egress control and an automatic kill switch into the product; in contracts, set notification deadlines against California's 15 days and New York's 72 hours, and state how many minutes from alert to stop and who has authority to stop. For investors: add a diligence question, "is your eval environment isolated from production?", and discount unclear answers by incident probability.
