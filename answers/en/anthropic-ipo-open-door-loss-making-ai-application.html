Will Anthropic's IPO open the door for loss-making AI-application and software companies to go public?

Asked by: Investor · Judged 2026-10-11 · Ledger updated 2026-11-25 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Original call (2026-10-11): It won't: even if Anthropic's listing goes well, loss-making VC-backed software companies (US-headquartered, listing on NYSE or Nasdaq, raising at least $100 million in the base offering) won't get an IPO window before the end of June 2027, and what holds them back is the gap between private and public valuations (both are inferences). In Q2 the IPO index rose 42% yet VC-backed tech largely remained absent; of 9 VC-backed IPOs in Q3, 8 were biotech and none was software (author's count).

Judgment

The consensus is the queue view: software companies are waiting for the frontier labs to list first. Renaissance's Matt Kennedy says what holds them back is market conditions and pricing, not Anthropic as a "black hole", yet also says a successful Anthropic offering would boost the companies queuing behind it. The diary's timing call is the direct opposite of that second half: even if Anthropic holds its offer price through its first quarter, these companies still won't get out before the end of June 2027; a lab ringing the bell removes the reason to queue, not the valuation gap. The gate of the 90 days after the first listing governs liquidity for the labs and their shareholders, not the IPO path for loss-making application-layer companies (on this basis ENTRY 145 corrects the scope of ENTRY 115); Anthropic itself is loss-making (per a draft prospectus Reuters saw, as relayed), so the comparison is which kind of loss the public market is willing to price. The author is a fund manager, and this call can also be read as pre-emptively lowering LPs' expectations for FutureX's own IPO distributions; discount it in both directions.

Facts

During SF Tech Week on the evening of 10/7, Nasdaq's head of new listings said investors' bar has risen (they want growth with cash efficiency) and software companies may wait until the frontier labs list; a Simpson Thacher partner at the same event said the bigger problem is that AI has pushed private valuations too high (BI, 10/8). The rate view: Renaissance (10/1) attributes these companies' absence to worries over AI spending, high bond yields and renewed rate hikes; the diary does not deny that rates matter, only notes that these companies were also largely absent in Q1 and Q2, when rates were lower. FRED GS10: the 10-year Treasury averaged 4.32% to 4.48% a month in Q2, and 4.99% in September. Renaissance's 3Q26 review: the 9 VC-backed IPOs raised $2.4 billion, and the only non-biotech one was a nuclear-fuel company. Motive publicly filed its S-1 on 2025-12-23, withdrew it on 2026-09-10 and the same day announced more than $1.3 billion of new funding; a public filing does not mean the window is open. Kennedy says buyers now demand an offering discount of about 20%, versus 10%–15% in the past (NY Post, 10/9).

What to do

For investors: do not time application-layer funds' IPO distributions to Anthropic's bell; wait for the next such company to publicly file an S-1 and price without breaking its range. A public filing comes at least 15 days before the roadshow, so this signal can be seen in advance. First ask GPs how much of their 2027 distributions rides on IPOs (the diary infers that application-layer funds of the 2020–2022 vintages will get their distributions through the end of June 2027 mainly from M&A and secondary sales, with IPO distributions close to zero). For founders: budget for no IPO before the end of June 2027, and move IPO-prep money to extending runway and preparing for M&A.
