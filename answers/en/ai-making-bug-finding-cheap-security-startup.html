AI is making bug-finding cheap: should a security startup still build vulnerability discovery, and where should the money go?

Asked by: Founder · Judged 2026-11-13 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Startups that only do the “finding” should stay out: bug-finding keeps getting cheaper, the engine sits with the frontier labs, and what is scarce is getting patches into devices that are hard to fix, so move the money to managed runtimes, embedded asset inventory and remote firmware patch delivery (retrospective, written 2026-11-13; event date 10/23). OpenAI reported on 11/4 that one Linux privilege-escalation exploit cost about $1,280 on average, with the patch generated alongside; Wiz said on 10/27 that a week on, about half of its affected customers had not finished patching.

Judgment

The consensus on 10/21–22 was that AI had dug up a bug reaching “70% of websites” (company claim) and that discovery was the new gold mine; by 11/13, “patching is the bottleneck” was also the consensus in the security press. The diary parts ways in only two places: money should be priced on whether the fix can be delivered, so it does not back companies that only find unknown bugs in third-party software; and while the “70% of websites” story put the damage on websites, the two compromises publicly confirmed by 11/13 both entered through security vendors' own devices. On money the disagreement is narrow: private markets in 2026 gave AI offensive-security companies valuations of $2 billion to $2.5 billion and up (secondary sources, unnamed), one of them an autonomous penetration-testing company on the validation side the diary does not warn off; the diary found no dated source of a pure discovery company valued above $1 billion. The endnote asks for discounting in both directions: “the engine sits with the frontier labs” favours the positioning of Anthropic, itself on the discovery side, and “do not discount hardware security vendors' valuations” touches Palo Alto Networks, an Anthropic partner.

Facts

PHP fixed two RCEs on 10/21 (CVSS v4.0 9.2), and the reporters all used frontier models; early signs had already surfaced in a 2024 manual audit. OpenAI said the same day it was offering $1 billion in subsidised credits to under-resourced defenders (company claim). OpenAI on 11/4: $1,213,430 in API fees yielded 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits, $1,279 each by the company's count including failed attempts (about $359 each counting only successful attempts), and the same pipeline generated 948 patches; a July run cost about $3,095 each, a drop of about 59% by October (both self-computed), with a different model and batch, so read it as direction only. Wiz (part of Google) on 10/27: fewer than 53% of the initially affected organisations were still vulnerable (customer telemetry, company claim; likely optimistic, by inference). The two compromises publicly confirmed by 11/13: Palo Alto Networks' PAN-OS, about 25 devices rooted on 10/23–24 (researchers' count, secondary, a small number; confirmed by Unit 42, which says patched customers were not affected); and Check Point gateways hit from 10/30, gateways that were still unpatched about a week after the fix was released.

What to do

For founders: stop building pure discovery of unknown bugs in third-party software, and skip patch generation too, since OpenAI's pipeline already generates the patches; move to verifying exposure, ranking fixes and delivering patches inside the customer's environment, or to managed runtimes (tracking versions automatically), embedded asset inventory and firmware patch orchestration, and products that can push patches or virtual patches remotely, with virtual patches paired with origin isolation. Autonomous penetration testing with known bugs is outside the “don't crowd in” call, though per-bug or per-hour pentest pricing is likely to be squeezed (inference). For investors: do not back companies that only find unknown bugs in third-party software; do not discount hardware security vendors' valuations, and in diligence ask how many days a patch takes to reach firmware and how long customers take to install it.
