Frontier models score near zero on GDPx finance and legal tasks: how should a legal or finance AI startup present its moat to investors?

Asked by: Founder · Judged 2026-11-13 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

Do not pitch GDPx's near-zero scores as a moat certificate; pitch the client's acceptance sheet: the near-zero scores are only a public reading of what models still cannot do, refreshed with every new model, so a moat that bets on models being unable to do the work needs an expiry date, while for a moat that bets on the client's acceptance sheet, stronger models turn into gross margin first (retrospective, written 2026-11-13; event date 11/4). In practice: turn the acceptance criteria into a private evaluation, write it into contracts, charge per delivery that passes acceptance, rerun it with the latest model every quarter and show it to investors next to the GDPx score for your domain; in the 11/9 data the best binary score in finance was still 2.78%, and no model passed a single legal task.

Judgment

The mainstream read the 12% and the zeros as a sign that agents are still far from replacing high-value white-collar work, and the market treats the near-zero binary scores in finance and legal as a safety cushion for vertical AI (an inference); that scores will rise quickly is SemiAnalysis's own expectation (a new version every 3–6 months), and counts as consensus too. Where the diary differs is the valuation reading: the near-zero scores are a public expiry reading on the moat, and valuations should be pegged to this board. The basis: the task writers are all RL-environment startups whose business is selling training tasks to labs, and labs pay $5,000 or more for a single high-quality task; each task writer publishes only one sample task, so labs cannot reach the test itself but can buy similar tasks, and the binary readings will therefore leave the floor (an inference). In the 11/9 refresh the overall score rose from 12% to 16.4% in 7 days while the finance and legal binary scores did not move, so the diary does not say the moat is expiring faster, only that its term is now readable and should be priced. If scores rise on purchased tasks of the same kind, that does not necessarily mean delivery in real client scenarios (an inference), which is why the check is the client's private evaluation, not GDPx alone.

Facts

SemiAnalysis released GDPx on 11/2: 206 tasks across 11 domains, and the top overall score at launch was 12%; the overall score is an equal-weighted average of the 11 domains, so it cannot be read as "completing 12% of tasks". All 24 finance tasks come from a replica data room of a single PE deal, and the 20 legal tasks cover 8 practice areas: it reads transactional finance and corporate legal delivery, not every legal or finance AI use case. 11/9 data: the best binary score in finance is 2.78% (2 passes in 72 attempts, self-computed), and all 21 models score 0 in legal; the best partial score in finance is 54.59%, from a model whose binary finance score is 0; the best partial score in legal is 38.44%. The task count is small: one pass in finance adds about 1.39 points (self-computed), so one or two lucky passes can make the reading jump.

What to do

For founders: lead investors will ask for the two kinds of revenue to be split (an inference), so split them yourself: put an expiry date on the part earned because general models cannot do the work, and present the part charged per delivery that passes acceptance on gross margin; a private evaluation alone is not enough, it has to be written into contracts and pricing. Model makers sell legal and finance industry products themselves (Anthropic's S-1/A of 11/10; OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services on 9/10, per a VentureBeat headline), so companies that charge on results will face price pressure too, and "stronger models turn into gross margin first" holds only while the acceptance criteria and the contracts stay with the company (an inference). For investors: discount revenue that depends on models being unable to do the work over a finite term, which is how to price a new round; in diligence you may ask the company to run its own private evaluation with the latest frontier model, updated every quarter, next to the GDPx reading for the same domain.
