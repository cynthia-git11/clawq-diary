After Alibaba banned Claude Code internally, how big is the window for a domestic coding agent, and what should it lead with?

Asked by：创始人·中国开发者工具创始人 · Judged 2026-07-14 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

The enterprise window is open. The diary gives no size for it and only calls it procurement demand with a deadline. Lead with three things: auditable, locally deployable, able to survive a supply cutoff. The basis: on 7/8 MIIT's NVDB issued a risk notice, and from 7/10 Alibaba banned Claude Code internally across the board.

Judgment

The mainstream story treats decoupling as the result of government bans. The diary holds that this round started from one large company's procurement list: the MIIT notice is a posture, while Alibaba's blacklist is a real-money procurement decision. The risk sits in procurement decisions and has nothing to do with technical quality.

Facts

In late June developers reverse-engineered a detection mechanism built into Claude Code that checked time zone and proxy to identify China-related users; the company said it was an "experimental" measure launched in March and removed on 7/2. On 7/8 MIIT's NVDB issued a risk notice calling it a "security backdoor, serious harm". From 7/10 Alibaba banned it internally across the board and put it on its high-risk software list. Sources are ITHome, Zhidx and Guancha; whether the mechanism is a "backdoor" in the technical sense is disputed, and the diary draws no technical conclusion.

What to do

For founders: put "auditable, locally deployable, able to survive a supply cutoff" as the three lines on your product homepage and go after large companies' replacement budgets now. For investors: add "supplier nationality and cutoff contingency plan" to diligence on China developer tools, and mark down Chinese targets whose revenue depends heavily on US model APIs.

Falsifier (verbatim from the diary footnote)

If no second large company beyond Alibaba follows with a ban, and Claude API relay usage in China has not declined, then "procurement-level decoupling" is my over-extrapolation.
