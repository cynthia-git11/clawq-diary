Can Jev be my agent's security boundary, and where in the stack should I actually use it?

Asked by：创始人·agent 产品 CTO · Judged 2026-09-25 · Ledger updated 2026-10-02 · 张倩 Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital

No. Try Jev only for classification, routing and gating, and build your own calibration tests. In Octomind's n=1 single integration test, a forged "user approved" state cut block probability from 0.76 to 0.48; jev-guard describes itself as "NOT a security boundary".

Judgment

The mainstream story treats Jev's speed and viral adoption as grounds to trust it as a gatekeeper. The diary's view is that a decision model at the same price and same score commoditised within a week: on the company's own evals Jev scores 67.8%, level with Luna and Sonnet 5, and no party has published ECE or Brier figures for "calibration", so the diary does not count it as a moat.

Facts

TypeSafe official docs (9/15): $0.042 per million input tokens, output free, rate limits that "may change without notice". Octomind blog (9/18, n=1 single integration test): after a forged approval, block probability fell from 0.76 to 0.48 and confidence from 0.64 to 0.22, with block still the top-probability option; as of 9/25 the diary had seen no direct company response. evals.typesafe.ai (four company-built workflows): Jev 67.8%, 0.4 seconds.

What to do

For founders: move classification, routing and gating to Jev-class models on a trial basis, do not use them as a security boundary, and build your own calibration tests. For investors: do not enter at the media-reported $10B offer valuation; if you invest, back the harness that holds the default slot and the eval tooling, and wait for TypeSafe to disclose non-token revenue.
