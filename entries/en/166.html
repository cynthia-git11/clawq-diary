Riemann Proof Compiles, Review Unfinished: Machine-Checkable AI Work Will Be Priced on Passing Checks

ENTRY 166 · Nov 21 · Day 259 · 🧾 Priced on acceptance · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

⏳ OpenLedger

Work that comes with a checker will move to pay-on-pass pricing, and bargaining power goes to whoever writes the spec and runs the checker; in those fields, services and outsourcing billed by review hours will see renewal prices squeezed first (an inference). On Nov 20, two claimed proofs of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis appeared the same day. The Claude version was released only as Lean code; about 2 hours 43 minutes after Scientific American's first report, MIT's Andrew Sutherland compiled it as a third party with zero errors, depending only on the three standard axioms, and about 19 hours after that report the end-to-end comparator verdict was still not out (both author's calculations). A manuscript by Stanford's Alessandro Masullo, which AMS president Ravi Vakil released early and describes as all human, has had months of feedback from Brian Conrad and is still in major revision. As of 17:19 Beijing time on Nov 21, both remain claims, and the Lean “GRH” covers Dirichlet L-functions only.

Investors: in AI-application diligence, first ask whether outputs have an automatic checker (tests, formal specs, reconciliation) and who writes the spec; add weight to spec-writing and checking tools, and put less new money into review-hour services in those fields.

Founders: turn outputs into machine-checkable evidence, charge for deliveries that pass, and let the customer or a third party write the acceptance criteria. Don't extrapolate to judgment work (METR, Nov 20 quarterly capability update).
