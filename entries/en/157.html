After GDPx, Legal and Finance AI Moats Should Be Valued With an Expiry Date

ENTRY 157 · Nov 4 · Day 242 · 🗓 Retro · Moat clock · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-13] Investors in legal and finance vertical AI should not read GDPx's near-zero scores in those two domains as a moat certificate. They are a public reading of what general models still cannot do, refreshed with every new model, and valuations should hang on that clock. On the Nov 2 launch board the top overall score was 12% (Claude Opus 5.5; an equal-weighted average of 11 domains, not a task completion rate); Claude Fable 5.2, added on Nov 9, pushed it to 16.4%. In the same Nov 9 data, the best binary score in finance was still 2.78%, and no model passed a single legal task. The best partial score in finance is about 55%: models get about half the requirements right yet almost never deliver a finished product a client can use, and that last stretch is what vertical AI sells. The benchmark's task writers also sell training tasks to labs (SemiAnalysis, Nov 2), so the binary readings in finance and legal will leave the floor (an inference); the Nov 9 refresh just did not reach them.

Investors: discount revenue that depends on general models being unable to do the work over a finite term; move money to companies that turn clients' acceptance criteria into private evaluations, write them into contracts and charge per delivery that passes.

Founders: rerun your private evaluation with the latest frontier model every quarter and show it to investors next to the GDPx reading for your domain. Falsifier (counted from 2026-11-13, a retrospective, not a prior hit): if by 2027-05-13 the best default GDPx v1 score is still at or below 2.78% in finance and still 0% in legal, and at least one frontier model released after 2026-11-13 has been scored on v1, the author recants: the board did not reach finance and legal within six months, and the window is longer than this entry assumed (with no new model scored, the source is recorded as missing); any point before then with either domain at 10% or more verifies it; in between is pending.

Disclosure: this diary is written with Claude (Anthropic) tools. Anthropic models topped GDPx both at launch (Claude Opus 5.5, Nov 2) and after the Nov 9 update (Claude Fable 5.2); Anthropic sells legal and financial industry products and is going public, so this reading cuts both ways for it; read with a discount in both directions. Alphabet is an Anthropic shareholder, cloud compute supplier and main competitor; OpenAI and Meta are competitors; SemiAnalysis, which built and scores GDPx, is an Anthropic enterprise customer per a secondhand report. FutureX holds no direct or indirect stake in SemiAnalysis, Anthropic, Alphabet, OpenAI or Meta (confirmed by the author from FutureX's records). FutureX holds AiYu and Caseflood (legal AI) and PlatoX/ccMonet (AI finance software), which sit in the category discussed here; this entry does not evaluate them and is not a valuation or marking view on them. FutureX also holds Corgi, an AI insurance company; GDPx has no insurance capability domain (only three insurance-broker tasks inside its alignment domain), and this entry neither extends to insurance nor evaluates Corgi, and is not a valuation view on it. FutureX also holds ByteDance, Mistral and RWKV, which build foundation models not tested in GDPx, and Genspark, Dify and Simular, which stronger general models affect in both directions. It also holds Konvery (AI data labeling and training data) and Micro1 (AI talent matching and data platform), whose business is close to the task sellers described here, and Zoa Research (AI quantitative forecasting models), which maps to GDPx's separate quant-trading domain; this entry's finance conclusion covers only the finance domain (PE deal and transaction accounting work), does not extend to quant trading, and is neither an evaluation of nor a valuation view on any of these companies.
