After PHP2Shell, AI Makes Bug-Finding Cheap; Move the Money to Patch Delivery

ENTRY 151 · Oct 23 · Day 230 · 🗓 Retro: fixing beats finding · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-13] Startups that only find bugs should stay out: finding is getting cheaper, the engines sit with frontier labs, and what is scarce is getting patches into devices customers cannot fix. All three reporters of the two PHP RCEs fixed on Oct 21 used frontier models. OpenAI said on Nov 4 that each Linux privilege-escalation exploit it landed cost about $1,280 on average, failed attempts included, and that the same pipeline generated the patches. Wiz said on Oct 27 that a week after disclosure about half of its affected customers had not finished patching. As of Nov 13, both publicly confirmed compromises came in through security vendors' own appliances: about 25 PAN-OS devices on Oct 23–24 (reported from Oct 28; a small number) and Check Point gateways from Oct 30 (disclosed Nov 12; the gateways were still unpatched about a week after a fix was available).

Investors: move money to managed runtimes, embedded-asset inventory and remote firmware patch delivery, and pair virtual patching with origin isolation; don't cut hardware security vendors' valuations, but ask in diligence how many days an upstream fix takes to reach their firmware and how fast customers install it.

Founders: frontier labs already ship candidate patches with their findings (OpenAI, Nov 4), so build patch prioritization and patch delivery inside the customer's environment instead. Falsifier (from 2026-11-13, not counted as a prior call; window to 2027-04-30; each leg judged separately): the call is wrong if an independent discovery-only company (AI discovery of unknown vulnerabilities in third-party software, meaning open-source or vendor products rather than its own or its paying customers' code, as its main business; credited as finder in a 2026 CVE; not owned by a frontier lab or cloud provider) first discloses ARR of $25M or more, or if, at the 2027-04-30 close, at least five newly confirmed PHP2Shell intrusion campaigns are counted and appliances are the initial entry in under 50% of them. Autonomous pentesting with known vulnerabilities doesn't count. No such company by the deadline is recorded as not triggered, not as a hit; at least five campaigns with appliances at 50% or more verifies the second leg; fewer than five is an insufficient reading; final check around 2027-05-15.
