Cybercab Is Short of Deployment, Not Capacity; Discount Services Tied to Tesla's Fleet

ENTRY 159 · Nov 8 · Day 246 · 🗓 Retrospective: deployment gate · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-13] Robotaxi startups hoping to service Tesla's fleet should lower their expectations. On Nov 6 Musk said Tesla would wind down Texas Cybertruck production around mid-2027 to make room for more Cybercab production, but Cybercab is short of deployment, not capacity. Installed Cybercab capacity in Texas exceeds 125,000 a year (Oct 21, company claim), while Tesla had 1,080 autonomous vehicles of all models listed as active under its Texas DMV authorization as of Oct 29, about three days of that capacity (author's calculation). On its Oct 21 earnings call Tesla said the line was outpacing deployment and that it aims to start selling Cybercabs by year-end, while Musk said most would stay Tesla-owned (all from third-party transcripts). The Oct 22 10-Q says Tesla is developing dedicated cleaning, charging, teleoperation and fleet-management infrastructure for Robotaxi.

Founders: size demand by vehicles in service times daily rides per vehicle, not by factory capacity, and sell to fleets not operated by Tesla.

Investors: discount revenue that depends on Tesla's own fleet, and track growth in vehicles in service in the TxDMV report. Falsifier (counted from 2026-11-13): (1) if, by the 2028-02-15 check, Tesla's filings, quarterly updates or official earnings calls disclose that cleaning and maintenance, charging, teleoperation or fleet management for its Robotaxi fleet is mainly contracted out to third parties, the revenue discount and the advice to sell to fleets not operated by Tesla are withdrawn; and if, by the same check, Tesla or a third party discloses in an SEC filing, a listed company's announcement or an official press release a Robotaxi fleet cleaning and maintenance, charging-depot, remote-assistance or fleet-management contract with Tesla covering three or more Tesla Robotaxi cities (none so far), the advice to sell to fleets not operated by Tesla is withdrawn; (2) if, by the same check, they show cumulative Cybercabs delivered to individuals or third-party fleets at or above those kept in Tesla's own fleet (self-held share 50% or less; customer-owned cars that Tesla operates count as delivered), reliance on Musk's 'mostly Tesla-owned' is withdrawn; (3) if, by the 2027-11-15 check, Tesla says in the same official channels that Cybercab output or available vehicles, including battery or 4680 supply, limited Robotaxi deployment, the 'deployment, not capacity' call is withdrawn. Musk's posts on X are recorded, not used to judge. Each test is verified only if, at its check, the latest filing still describes dedicated Robotaxi infrastructure with no outsourcing or such service contract disclosed (1), the latest disclosure still shows over 50% self-held (2), or Tesla still attributes the limit to technology, regulation or operations (3); in between it is pending, and if Tesla gives no official figure or statement the test is logged as source missing.

Disclosure: written with Claude; SpaceXAI (formerly xAI), part of Musk-led SpaceX, both competes with Anthropic and supplies it with compute, Tesla owns SpaceX stock, and Cybercab runs Grok, so the author may read Musk's claims too harshly or too kindly; discount both ways. FutureX holds no direct or indirect stake in Tesla, SpaceX or Anthropic (confirmed by the author from FutureX's records).
