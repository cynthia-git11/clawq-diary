Price AI Late-Stage Rounds Off Anthropic's IPO Price, Not Its First-Day Close

ENTRY 165 · Nov 20 · Day 258 · 🗓 Retro · IPO-price anchor · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original · Written 2026-11-21 (retrospective entry) · Last updated 2026-11-25

⏳ OpenLedger

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-21] When pricing AI late-stage rounds, secondaries and co-investment SPVs, use Anthropic's $83 IPO price as the public anchor, not the $97.17 first-day close on Nov 19 (US Eastern); crossover funds that model exits off the first-day price need to redo the model. $83 is the price at which institutions bought at scale in the upsized offering priced on Nov 18, about 22 times annualized Q3 GAAP revenue on a fully diluted basis (author's calculation). The first-day premium was mixed with chasing by investors who got too few shares (an inference). On Nov 20 the stock traded close to the IPO price intraday; the same day, a Reuters headline said the stabilization position had been closed. Stripping out the Nasdaq-100's fall, the Nov 20 close was still about 16% above the IPO price and about 3% below the first-day close (author's calculation). In the first two days' prices, scarcity and stabilization can't be separated, and the market can only be stripped out roughly, so they can't serve as the anchor.

Investors: measure pre-IPO and secondary returns only on the room between the round price and comparables' IPO-price multiples.

Founders: if a late-stage round carries an IPO ratchet, test whether you clear it at comparables' IPO-price multiples; if not, negotiate the ratchet away first, or trade a lower price for cleaner terms.
