Nvidia Brings Back Jensen Huang: I Was Wrong, and Government Backing Should Be Valued as a Debt

ENTRY 168 · Nov 25 · Day 263 · 🏛 Backing as debt · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

⏳ OpenLedger

Entry 163 was wrong. I treated Washington as the gate and said shareholder letters and petitions could not change the outcome; Washington turned out to be the one that let him back in. On Nov 22 Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang (8-K), saying input from “senior levels of the United States government” left it confident there was no national-security risk. New call: the market prices this backing as a premium; I treat it as a debt. Congress and state attorneys general are beyond its reach, and the White House can still withdraw it or charge for it (an inference). Trump says he convinced the board; Huang denies he is back because of Trump. On Nov 23 NVDA rose 31.5% while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) rose 14.0%; from Huang’s Nov 11 resignation to Nov 24, NVDA fell 10.6% and SOX fell 16.4% (Nasdaq data, author’s calculations). Nvidia’s excess over SOX since the resignation day is mostly the price of the backing, and it will not hold through 2027-03-31 (an inference).

Investors: list political backing as its own line and discount it for withdrawal, pursuit by Congress and the states, and the government charging a price; in diligence, ask whether the board reached its national-security conclusion on its own or on the government’s assurance. Founders in chips and compute: keep “someone up top” out of your fundraising materials; investors will mark it down as a contingent liability.
