Weight Fingerprints Can’t Find the Data Center: Discount Overseas Data Centers’ China-Tenant Revenue by License Odds

ENTRY 162 · Nov 14 · Day 252 · 🗓 Retro · License discount · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original · Written 2026-11-21 (retrospective entry)

⏳ OpenLedger

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-21] Revenue that overseas data centers earn from Chinese tenants should be discounted by the odds of a US license, equity more than debt (an inference). On Nov 12 Unemployment Labs said six Chinese open models carry Blackwell traces, but the weights cannot show which data center the chips sat in; the same day @ramez said these runs are on the order of 1% of China’s in-country AI compute. From Nov 19 the new BIS rule checks the data center: serving China-headquartered companies above the threshold needs a case-by-case license, and existing data centers must get one by 2027-02-17 (author’s calculation). On the report’s best estimates, each run is about 4.6–15 times the threshold (author’s calculation).

Investors: carve out China-tenant revenue and discount it; if the Nov 12 rush to rent lifts utilization, keep that out of valuations. Lenders are asking for early-repayment clauses (Bloomberg, Nov 17–18, secondhand), which push the risk onto data-center shareholders (an inference). Put new money into US domestic capacity, or into non-China-tenant capacity outside Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil.

Founders: write the termination fees and prepayments lenders want into your leases, and swap the tenant if the license fails.
