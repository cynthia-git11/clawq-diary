Kimi's Ranking Doesn't Buy Moonshot's Own API Volume: On OpenRouter, Its Share Is Down to About a Tenth

ENTRY 152 · Oct 25 · Day 232 · 🗓 Retro · The till · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-13] Kimi's ranking does not buy Moonshot's own API volume. K3.1 Pro ranked 4th overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at launch on Oct 23. After the weights came out on Nov 4, Moonshot's own share of K3.1 Pro requests on OpenRouter fell from all of them to about a tenth by Nov 13 (author's calculation), volume down by well over half (an inference). On the volume hosts take, what Moonshot can collect rests on the license, published with the weights on Nov 4 and identical to K3's from July: hosts whose revenue with affiliates exceeds $20M a year must sign a separate agreement before commercial use, terms undisclosed. I wrote in ENTRY 112 that the money would come from Moonshot's own downstream API; I am revising that: on OpenRouter, its own API can't hold volume, and that tenth will keep falling.

Investors: split Moonshot's revenue into domestic subscriptions, own API and hosting revenue share, size own API on post-release volume, and pay no premium for rankings until revenue-share terms are disclosed.

Founders: building on K3.1, ask whether your host is a certified inference partner or has a separate agreement (license sections 2 and 4), and write throughput and SLA into the contract. Falsifier (from 2026-11-13; a retrospective call, not counted as a call made in advance): if Moonshot's own request share for K3.1 Pro on OpenRouter averages at least 9.6% (the current value) over Dec 25, 2026 to Jan 23, 2027, or if Moonshot discloses before Jan 23, 2027 that its overseas own-API revenue or volume after the Nov 4 weights release exceeds the pre-release level, the call is wrong; at most 7.3% (K3's own-endpoint reading, a different scope) with no contrary disclosure verifies it; in between is recorded as unresolved at expiry.

Disclosure: written with Claude (Anthropic) tools; K3.1 Pro is benchmarked against Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic has accused Moonshot of distilling Claude, and its Nov 10 S-1/A cites open-weight models as pricing pressure; Amazon is a major Anthropic investor, and AMD and Nvidia have announced or committed investments in it; FutureX holds Hugging Face (host of the weights; Nvidia's announced acquisition has not closed), Mistral, RWKV and ByteDance, and holds no direct or fund-level indirect stake in Moonshot, Anthropic, Amazon, AMD, Nvidia or Alibaba (confirmed by the author from FutureX's records). Read at a discount both ways: the conclusion suits closed labs including Anthropic, and FutureX's Hugging Face stake sits on the open-weight distribution side.
