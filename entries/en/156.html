AI Detectors Are Entering Publishing's Submission Checks, and Authors Pay for False Positives

ENTRY 156 · Nov 2 · Day 240 · 🗓 Retrospective: style referee · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-13] The AI detection business will be won or lost on whether buyers drop a detector after one public false positive; the average accuracy vendors advertise will not settle it. Individual false flags are still being argued over, yet publishers and agents have started screening submissions with detectors, and authors bear the cost of wrong flags. Pangram reports a 0.009% false-positive rate (Oct 16, company claim); independent evaluations mostly sit near zero, the highest being 2.0% on a 2025 version with a small sample. Readings from before the event: on Oct 30 Bloomberg reported authors dropping em dashes and adding deliberate typos (secondhand, via Techmeme's summary); on Oct 31 NPR interviewed a ghostwriter who, after being flagged, changed wording and used fewer commas. Since Aug 24, on the author's instruction to remove AI feel, this diary's own style contract has capped not-A-but-B lines and chained dash clauses: the same kind of conforming this entry describes, though the diary has always stated that it is written with Claude.

Investors: avoid humanizers and anti-detection tools, which can go to zero when platform rules change; back the provenance and proof layer (detection, signing, audit) and value it on institutional renewal rates and independent false-positive rates, not consumer traffic.

Founders: sign annual contracts with publishers and platforms first, and let third parties test your false-positive rate. Falsifier (counted from 2026-11-13, final check 2027-05-13): if Quora, Substack, LessWrong or Internet Archive (institutional Pangram users named in Menlo Ventures' Jul 29 post), or any Big Five US publisher, publicly drops or rules out detection scores citing false positives or accuracy, the claim that publishers and agents have started screening with detectors and that authors bear the cost of wrong flags is withdrawn along with the allocation call; if a newly published independent evaluation of Pangram 4 or later, on at least 1,000 human texts written before 2022-11-30 (or with process evidence of human authorship), finds an overall false-positive rate of 0.1% or more with at least three texts misflagged, the valuation yardstick and the allocation call are withdrawn. It counts as verified only if neither happens, at least three of those four users still use Pangram (per their own pages or, failing that, a current Pangram or Menlo customer listing, a company claim), and at least one qualifying independent evaluation comes in below 0.1%; otherwise it is recorded as not falsified.

Disclosure: written with Claude; Pangram's seed round was led by Menlo Ventures through the Anthology Fund that Menlo set up with Anthropic, Pangram says it trains its detector on Anthropic model outputs, and Anthropic itself builds watermarking (since Aug 11) and provenance tools (FlashProve, Nov 2 US time), so the call to back provenance runs in Anthropic's favor, while the authors-pay framing favors people who write with Claude, this diary included; discount it both ways. FutureX holds no direct or indirect stake in Pangram or Anthropic (confirmed by the author from FutureX's records).
