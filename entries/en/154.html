Labs Give Away Complexity Proofs, Anthropic Keeps the Drug Program

ENTRY 154 · Oct 29 · Day 236 · 🗓 Retro · proofs for free · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-13] For AI-for-science companies, “our model can make discoveries” now deserves a valuation discount. On Oct 26 (US time) OpenAI and Anthropic each released, free and on the same day, proofs they say refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH); on Oct 27 Anthropic disclosed that it now owns a drug program outright. Each lab says its model found the core result on Oct 16, by different methods; Anthropic's paper concedes its proven bound beats brute-force search only when n exceeds 10^10000 (for formulas with 5n clauses). The program came from Mithril, a one-person company acquired in July: its founder used Claude to coordinate 11 outside labs and took a muscular-dystrophy antibody to mouse data in under 18 months. All company claims, not peer reviewed as of Nov 13.

Investors: check first whether a company holds patented molecules, its own lab data or a working outsourced-lab network; discount platforms that sell only discovery capability, on the assumption that frontier labs will do the work themselves; Mithril's price was not disclosed, so do not set exit values on being bought by a lab.

Founders: put molecules, patents and data on the first page of the deck, the model demo in the appendix. Labs give away proofs and keep molecules. Falsifier (from 2026-11-13, through 2027-06-30; earlier events don't count as a hit), testing three judgments in the text: (1) behind “released, free and on the same day”: this kind of proof is no longer something only one lab can produce. Withdrawn if either lab retracts its ETH proof or concedes an unfixable gap, if the public examination organized by Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams (invited by OpenAI, covering OpenAI's proof only) finds a fatal gap in OpenAI's proof in writing, or if a rebuttal in a peer-reviewed venue finds an unfixable gap in either. Verified only if both stand and each clears: OpenAI's accepted by a peer-reviewed venue (STOC, FOCS, SODA, JACM) or cleared in that group's written report (a shepherd group OpenAI invited, read as a data point, not certification), and Anthropic's accepted by such a venue; otherwise pending, adjudication source missing, not counted. (2) behind “keep molecules”: drug assets are now a frontier-lab acquisition target. Withdrawn if by 2027-06-30 OpenAI, Anthropic or Google DeepMind has completed no acquisition, announced after 2026-11-13, of a company holding molecules or preclinical or clinical drug assets, or of such assets directly (licensing and co-development don't count); the count since the start is 0 (Mithril, before the start, is 1), so the status quo leads to withdrawal. (3) the lead judgment: withdrawn if at least 2 AI-for-science companies with no disclosed drug assets, wet lab or own experimental data close rounds at $1B+ valuations no lower than their previous round (a first priced round counts as not lower); fewer than 2 verifies it.

Disclosure: This diary is written with Claude (Anthropic) tools; Anthropic is judged here and was mid-roadshow for its IPO when this was written (roadshow from Nov 10, pricing expected Nov 18, per its Nov 12 FWP, which lists drug discovery among its target markets), so read with a discount in both directions; OpenAI is Anthropic's direct competitor; Mithril Medicine was acquired by Anthropic; Google is an Anthropic investor and compute supplier and competes with it through Gemini and Google DeepMind. FutureX holds no direct or indirect stake in OpenAI, Anthropic, Mithril Medicine or Alphabet (confirmed by the author from FutureX's records), and holds Great Bay Bio, an AI-driven biologics CMC platform whose field overlaps the manufacturing-document work Anthropic describes in its Oct 27 post; this entry does not judge it; read with a discount in both directions.
