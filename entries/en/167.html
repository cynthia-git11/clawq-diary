OpenAI's Chief Scientist Proposes Perplexity-Based AI Speed Limits; Whoever Holds the Ruler Issues the Licenses

ENTRY 167 · Nov 23 · Day 261 · 🗓 Retro · Who holds the ruler · Cynthia Zhang · FutureX Capital · 中文原文 · Chinese original · Written 2026-11-25 (retrospective entry)

⏳ OpenLedger

[Retrospective, written 2026-11-25] Model-layer investors should watch the ruler: whoever's test set the speed limit is measured on is the one issuing licenses, and catch-up labs that borrow the ruler pay extra to prove compliance. On Nov 22 (US Eastern), OpenAI proposed common limits based on perplexity and other metrics, with no number and no date. Its own log-perplexity figures: 0.761 for its released model and 0.732 for an internal model, measured on its own codebase. As of Nov 25, the only other company we found reporting a number was Microsoft: 0.819 (its own figure), with OpenAI's permission and on the same tokenizer. A limit binds first whoever is closest to it, so half of the effect helps catch-up labs; the other half is the cost of proving compliance and borrowing the ruler.

Investors: add one line to diligence: if the limit is set on someone else's test set, how far is the company from the limit, how costly is the proof, and can it borrow the ruler at all; if labs hold the ruler, money moves toward neutral evaluation (an inference).

Founders: OpenAI said on Nov 18 that it will slow releases, so don't bet the roadmap on the next model shipping on time. The author bets that by the end of November 2027, a US, EU or UK regulatory text or a written commitment by three or more frontier labs will make this kind of metric a limit.
